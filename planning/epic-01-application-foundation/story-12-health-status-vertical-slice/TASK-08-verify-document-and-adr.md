## Work

- **Re-run all six acceptance criteria against the shipping tree and the deployed environment.** Re-take every figure rather than citing one. The record across ten stories is that the half that is cheap to check has never been wrong and the half that cannot be checked has been wrong nearly every time it is read
- **Write `docs/adr/0012-*`.** It is the twelfth and it should record what this story decided rather than what it built: the client-side status vocabulary and why it is not `HealthStatus`; what "degraded" means and why that definition and not another — including the **reversal trigger** Task 1.12.1 attached to rejecting latency, which is a `/health` that does real work, and the consequence that a timeout is `unreachable`; **one indicator or two — decided by Task 1.12.4 and it is _two_**, on the argument that `FeedStatus` is a fact the backend _reports_ about the market feed while `BackendStatus` is one the client _concludes_ about whether the backend answered at all, that they fail independently, and that a live feed behind an unreachable backend is a real state one indicator would have to pick between; **what is shared is the marker language rather than the component, and the cost of that — two stylesheets holding one idiom by imitation — belongs in the record beside the decision**; **the fourth visual case that is deliberately not a fourth state**, which is `hasChecked` and the `checking` placeholder, and why rendering the honest `unreachable` there would report the client's own startup as a fact about the server while rendering nothing would collapse the region and be indistinguishable from a broken indicator; **whether a user sees which cause degraded the backend**, which is decided as a per-cause sentence rather than a slug or a `title` attribute, with the tooltip rejected for the reason Task 1.4.5 rejected one; **why the last successful check is an absolute hand-formatted clock time shown in every state except `healthy`**, and why `toLocaleTimeString` was rejected on a width that `tabular-nums` cannot fix; how much of a `requestId` a user sees — **which here is none at all, and structurally so, since no prop can carry one**; the polling interval and the three costs behind it — **including that it is bounded below by the client's five-second deadline, which ~~nothing checks~~ a test in `use-backend-health.test.ts` asserts since Task 1.12.3, and that the interval was deliberately kept a literal because a number coupled to another literal cannot be made configurable on its own without letting somebody invert an ordering from a file where the other half is invisible**; that a hidden tab does not poll, and what that costs anything trying to observe the loop; the client's seven outcomes and why they collapse to three states, since `api-error` and `http-error` differ only by whether a user has an id to quote and `aborted` is deliberately not a state; how the `requestId` decision constrains `ErrorFallback` without changing it; and what a green indicator does and does not certify — which is the same shape as ADR 0010's "what the tick certifies" and is the honest framing here, because every state in this story is a client-side conclusion
