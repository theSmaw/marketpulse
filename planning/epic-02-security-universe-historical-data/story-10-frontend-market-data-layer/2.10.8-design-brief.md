# Design brief — Task 2.10.8, every state produced rather than described

**For:** the UX and design team
**Subject:** `BarSeriesPanel`, on `/securities` and `/securities/:symbol`
**Task:** [TASK-08](TASK-08-every-state-produced-not-described.md) — the last
build task of [Story 2.10](STORY.md)
**Prepared:** 2026-09-10
**Delivered against it:** [`2.10.8-design.html`](2.10.8-design.html), in this
folder

The bar-series panel already renders six states. What it cannot yet say is that
the numbers on it are one request out of date, that a security has dropped out
of the universe, or anything at all to a screen reader. This brief is the design
and content work that closes those three gaps.

Six deliverables, **D1 to D6**, in §3. Everything before them is context;
everything after them is the fence.

---

## 1. What the panel is

It is the first thing in this product to render a _series_, and it renders it as
**stated facts rather than a drawing**. A headline close and change, a coverage
sentence, the four prices as a metric strip, the window asked for above the
window actually held, the bar count, and which market feed the bars came from.

**There is no line, no candle, no axis and no sparkline**, and that is a scope
decision rather than an unfinished state. Story 2.12 owns charting, and a small
drawing added here would be that decision taken by accident, on the smallest
possible evidence, by whoever needed one first.

Its job is to make the data layer's correctness _visible_ while it is still
cheap to be wrong about. Every fact on it is one a person can check against the
store. That is also why the two windows are always both on screen: _"we hold
1,438 of the 1,950 minutes you asked for, through 15:42"_ is the honest shape of
this product's most common answer, and a panel showing only the data would
quietly answer a narrower question than the one that was asked.

The panel receives one prop — a six-member discriminated union — and renders the
member it is given. That union is the vocabulary this brief works in.

| State     | Kind              | What is on screen                                                      | Control             |
| --------- | ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| `loading` | —                 | A sentence and four ragged skeleton bars                               | None                |
| `loaded`  | Answer            | Every bar of the window asked for, and the coverage line says so       | None                |
| `partial` | Answer            | How many bars we hold and through when, from the response              | None                |
| `empty`   | Answer            | A 200 with nothing in the window, and the likeliest reason             | None                |
| `refused` | About the request | The server's own sentence, verbatim — cap, calendar, or unknown symbol | **Never**           |
| `failed`  | Failure           | Whether waiting helps, and the correlation id                          | Retry, if retryable |

### Already built — do not redesign it

All six renderings exist and are reviewable side by side in
`BarSeriesPanel.stories.tsx`'s `AllPermutations` grid. The refusals carry no
control and no correlation id; the retryable failure carries both; the
incoherent one says asking again will not help. The shared retry control was
extracted at the 2026 design refresh and is now `components/Button` in all three
places that used to hand-style one — TASK-08's bullet asking for that extraction
is **superseded** and should not be worked.

Three further things are settled and are not open here: the refusals render the
server's `message` word for word, a refusal never acquires a retry button, and
the panel is a `Panel` with square corners on the cool ground.

## 2. The three gaps

Everything below follows from three facts about the panel as it stands today,
and each one is a thing the screen is currently silent about.

1. **A cached series paints before the fresh one arrives, and nothing marks it.**
   The behaviour is deliberate and already shipped: a held series for the same
   request paints in the first commit while the request behind it is still in
   flight. Because every cache read is accompanied by a request, that can only
   last for the duration of one request — but for that duration a person is
   reading numbers with no way to know a newer answer is coming.

2. **There is a rendering in shipped code that nothing has ever executed.** Three
   of the six states carry a `securityStatus`, and when it reads `untracked` the
   panel shows a note. No fixture, no test, no story and no browser cause
   produces it. It is not a member of the union — it is a **field on three of
   them**, which is exactly the shape a states checklist walks past.

3. **The panel says nothing to a screen reader, on purpose.** It is deliberately
   not a live region, because the question of what an asynchronously-filled
   surface announces when its content changes _more than once_ belongs to this
   task rather than to one component. It now has to be answered on a page that
   already has a live region in it.

## 3. What we need from you

### D1 — The stale mark

**Say that a newer answer is on its way, without spoiling the one on screen.**

The hardest of the six, and the reason this brief exists. Design the mark that
says _a request is in flight behind these numbers_.

Two properties make it tractable. **Only an answer is ever stale** — the cache
holds `loaded`, `partial` and `empty` and never a failure or a refusal, so there
is no stale-failure case to design. And a **different** request never shows the
previous one's series: a symbol change resets the panel, so the thing being
marked is always the same security, timeframe and window as the answer coming to
replace it.

- The mark itself: its form, its weight and where it sits — with the coverage
  line, on the subject header, or across the whole body.
- Whether the headline close and change are touched at all, and the argument
  either way. The standing rule is that **motion must never make a number harder
  to read**; a treatment that dims or blurs a price is the same failure by
  another route.
- The transition _out_ of stale, at the moment the fresh answer lands —
  including the case where the fresh answer is identical to the held one, which
  is the common case for a closed session and where nothing visible changes at
  all.
- A motion spec in tokens only: `--motion-duration-quick` 120ms,
  `--motion-duration-settle` 240ms, one standard ease, and 0ms under reduced
  motion (answered at the token layer, so do **not** write a media query).
- The words. One short phrase, and it must not imply the numbers are wrong —
  they are correct, they are simply one request old.
- A greyscale proof of whatever you land on.

> **See it in two clicks.** `/securities/NVDA` → `/securities/AMD` → back. The
> third view paints a cached answer in its first commit with a request still in
> flight. No throttling and no interception needed.

### D2 — `untracked`

**Review the note nobody has ever seen.**

An untracked security is one MarketPulse holds bars for and no longer follows.
It is not a 404 and it is not a warning: the bars are real, the series is
correct, and what changed is the universe. The current note reads:

> MarketPulse no longer tracks this security. These bars are what was stored
> while it did.

— rendered under the provenance line, with a ring marker.

- Confirm or replace the copy, seen against a real populated series rather than
  read in isolation.
- Decide where it belongs: a note in the body, where it currently sits below
  everything, or a mark on the subject header beside the symbol, where it
  qualifies the whole panel. It is a property of the **security**, not of this
  answer.
- Say how it composes with a partial coverage line and with D1's stale mark,
  since all three can be true at once.

### D3 — Two live regions on one page

**Decide what a page with two speaking surfaces does.**

`/securities` now fills two things over the network: the tracked-universe table,
which already owns a polite live region, and this panel, which owns none. Adding
one makes it the page's **second**, and two polite regions updated in the same
moment are queued by a screen reader in an order neither component controls.
Landing on the page would announce two things about two different subjects.

- Pick one: a single region for the page; two regions whose sentences each name
  their own subject; or the panel staying silent while the table speaks. Give
  the reason, because the next asynchronous surface inherits it.
- If the panel speaks, its sentences must name the security — a region that says
  _"1,438 bars"_ next to one describing 518 securities is two facts with no
  subjects.

> **Constraint.** The panel is a `role="status"` or it is nothing.
> `role="alert"` belongs to the render-failure fallback and the browser suite
> asserts that distinction on every route.

### D4 — Announcement copy

**A sentence per state, and an answer for the second one.**

The universe table settled the mechanism: a persistent region rendered in every
state, never unmounted, silent on arrival — because arriving at a page is not a
change, and a region added at the same moment as its content is not reliably
announced. What is new here is that the content changes _more than once_: the
symbol changes on a navigation today, and the window will change from Story
2.13.

That raises a question a page filling once could not. It was measured on a retry
and it is a mechanism rather than a matter of taste: **a live region whose text
does not change announces nothing.** Any two consecutive states producing the
same sentence are silent, and the case nobody pictures is the one that matters —
a retry or a refetch that lands back on the state it started from. On the
universe page that made a second identical failure completely inaudible; the fix
was a distinct in-between sentence the region passes through and back out of.

- A sentence for each of the six states, plus stale and untracked.
- What is said when a _second_ series replaces a first, and whether a
  stale-then-fresh sequence speaks once or twice.
- A decision on the re-entrant case: a refetch that lands on an identical
  answer. Either an in-between sentence that makes the return audible, or
  deliberate silence **with the reason written down** — _nothing changed_ is a
  defensible answer here, but it has to be chosen rather than inherited.
- A note on rate: a region that spoke on every keystroke of Story 2.11's search
  would be actively hostile, and that story inherits whatever you write.

### D5 — Everyday copy

**Final pass on the two states this product actually shows.**

Measured 2026-09-10 against both the local and the deployed store: a one- or
two-session window comes back `empty`, because the current session has not
opened and the nightly backfill has not taken the one before it; five sessions
comes back `partial`. **`empty` and `partial` are what this panel renders in
normal use** — they are the default view, not the edge cases, which makes their
copy the most-read text on the surface.

Current copy, `empty`:

> No bars stored for this window.
>
> We asked for 4 Sep 09:30 – 4 Sep 16:00 and hold nothing in it. A window
> reaching into the current session is usually this: stored history is caught up
> overnight.

Current copy, `partial` — every number from the response:

> Holding 1,438 bars, through 15:42 — less than the window asked for, which runs
> to 16:00.

- Confirm or improve both, and the complete-window sentence beside them. The
  complete case says so out loud rather than staying silent, because silence
  makes _"we hold all of it"_ and _"nobody checked"_ look identical.
- Keep `empty` readable as an **answer**. A person who reads it as a broken
  product or a delisted stock is the failure mode; both are wrong.

> **The highest-leverage sentence on the surface.** The CI browser gate runs
> against a store with 518 securities and **zero bars**, deliberately — a
> backfill is metered and the gate has no credentials. So every window on CI is
> `empty`, which makes it the one state the gate can produce for free on every
> route, and the one piece of copy an automated check can hold us to. `partial`
> and `loaded` cannot be produced in the gate at all; their browser coverage is
> a developer's machine and the deployed check.

### D6 — What we need handed back

- **Annotated states** covering all six members plus stale and untracked, drawn
  at the product's real tokens — the idiom `story-10-design.html` in this
  folder already uses. Redlines in **token names**, not in pixel values.
- **The copy matrix** as a plain table: state → visible copy → announced
  sentence. It is pasted into the task file as the record, so write it to be
  read there.
- **The motion note** for D1, in token terms only.
- **Greyscale captures** of any state whose new mark carries meaning.
- Flag anything that implies a **new shared component**. Anything landing under
  `src/components/` owes a stories file, and `pnpm verify` fails without one.

## 4. Constraints that outrank taste

Each of these has caught a real defect, here or on the screen before this one.
They are stated in
[`VISUAL-LANGUAGE.md`](../../epic-01-application-foundation/story-04-ui-component-library-and-styling-conventions/VISUAL-LANGUAGE.md)
and in `CLAUDE.md`, and they are not this task's to reverse.

| #      | Rule                                           | Why                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ------ | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **R1** | Colour is never the sole encoding              | Under `grayscale(1)` the positive green and the negative red differ by **1.04:1** — hue is the entire difference. Every mark carries a shape, a sign, a glyph or a word as well  |
| **R2** | The crimson accent never touches a datum       | It has exactly four permitted positions, all in the chrome. It sits a hair from the negative-price red, and on a day the market falls a second red is a coin flip for the reader |
| **R3** | Motion must never make a number harder to read | A value that fades or slides while an analyst reads it is worse than one that changes instantly. Reduced motion resolves the durations to 0ms at the token layer                 |
| **R4** | No copy carries a figure                       | Every number on screen comes from the response. A sentence written around a specific count is wrong for every window but one — we have already corrected one derived figure      |
| **R5** | No chart and no window control                 | Story 2.12 owns charting; Story 2.13 owns the window control and its calendar rules. A second window is needed here only to _produce_ a transition, and a URL edit does that     |
| **R6** | A marker with no colour is invisible           | `Marker` owns no colour and reads one off its context. A state that adds a marker and forgets the property renders nothing — no error, correct DOM, nothing in `verify` sees it  |
| **R7** | No test can assert colour                      | No stylesheet is applied in the test environment, structurally. Only the browser suite sees the stale treatment and the contrast                                                 |
| **R8** | The bar is a standing instruction              | It must read as a real funded product, look designed rather than defaulted, and feel alive. Visual quality is an acceptance criterion on this task, not polish deferred          |

## 5. How to see every state

Nothing here needs a backend, a database or a market-data credential. Ten
recorded response bodies live in `apps/frontend/src/fixtures/`, eight of them
taken from the real endpoint over the real store, and the workshop renders the
panel from any of them:

```
pnpm --filter @marketpulse/frontend storybook
```

Then **Components → BarSeriesPanel → AllPermutations**.

| Cause                                 | Fixture                | State                   |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------- |
| A complete answer                     | `full`                 | `loaded`                |
| A window past what the store holds    | `partial`              | `partial`               |
| A window with no prints in it         | `empty`                | `empty`                 |
| History plus a live tail              | `stitched`             | `loaded`, two sources   |
| A window over the bar cap             | `refusedCap`           | `refused`               |
| A window outside the trading calendar | `refusedCalendar`      | `refused`               |
| A symbol the universe does not hold   | `refusedUnknownSymbol` | `refused`               |
| The store unreachable                 | `unavailable`          | `failed`, **retryable** |
| Our own server contradicting itself   | `incoherent`           | `failed`, not retryable |
| A feed slug this bundle does not know | `unknownFeed`          | `failed`                |

Two of the eight states you are designing for have no recorded body yet, and
producing them is ours rather than yours. **Stale** is a property of the
transport, reachable through D1's two-click route. **Untracked** needs an
eleventh recording, produced either by dropping a symbol from the universe file
and re-running the loader — which untracks the row rather than deleting it — or
by setting the status directly for a one-off. Either way it is a **populated**
answer with a different status, because an untracked security keeps its bars and
the route still serves them.

> **Do not construct a state by hand.** Every fixture is put through the real
> transition function before it reaches a story, so a story cannot render a
> state the layer cannot produce. A hand-built `partial` whose coverage
> disagrees with its bars is unreachable in the real product, and a treatment
> tuned against one renders the real thing wrongly.

## 6. Done when

- The stale-while-loading treatment is decided, marked without relying on colour
  alone, and does not make any number harder to read — with the decision and its
  **reversal trigger** written down, because Story 2.13's window control and
  Epic 3's live feed both inherit it rather than deciding it again.
- Every one of the eight states has been seen on screen from a **named cause**,
  and the causes are recorded so the next person reproduces rather than
  re-invents.
- The two-live-region question has one answer, with its reason.
- A state that is re-entered — a retry or a refetch landing where it started —
  is either still announced or recorded as deliberately silent.
- The copy matrix exists as a table that can be pasted into the record.

## 7. Out of scope, stated so it is not taken in good faith

The charting decision (Story 2.12). A control that changes the window (Story
2.13). Search, and the per-security route's URL strings (Story 2.11). Provenance
as a product-wide requirement, including a series that truthfully names two
feeds — that arrives with Epic 3's IEX socket and Story 2.14 owns the wording.
Any change to the six states' shape: the union is the data layer's, and a
seventh member is an engineering decision this brief does not ask you to force.
